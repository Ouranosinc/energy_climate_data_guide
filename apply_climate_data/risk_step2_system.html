Step 2: Understand the System, Structure, and Components

This step builds a clear, practical understanding of the system being assessed, in the context of climate change. Teams should review how the system is designed, built, operated, maintained, and how it influences other systems to deliver its intended service. This understanding helps the assessment stay focused on the parts of the systems that matter most for safety, performance, and continuity, and it provides the system information needed to assess climate hazards and exposure. The level of detail should match the scope and objective confirmed previously. For a simple assessment, the system may be a single component. For a system-wide or higher-risk assessment, the system may include multiple critical structures, components, sub-components, and supporting services.

NoteInputs

Typical inputs for this step include engineering drawings and design basis information, system specifications, operating procedures, maintenance strategies, condition and inspection reports, operational experience, and prior risk or event documentation (such as near misses).

Actions for this step may include reviewing the available documentation, walking down the system where practical, and engaging station staff and subject matter experts (SMEs) to confirm how it works in practice, how it is maintained, what has changed since installation, and what current condition and age imply for performance. This step also includes checking for “design intent versus current reality” and avoiding assumptions that aging systems will continue to perform as originally specified, even in the face of a changing climate.

NoneOutcomes

Outcomes of this step may include:

  • Documented understanding of the system and how it works with other systems to deliver its intended service, written at a level of detail that fits the assessment scope and decision need
  • List of systems, structures and components critical aspects that will be included in the assessment
  • For each critical aspect identified for the system, the output typically captures key specifications, known condition issues, relevant operating experience (OPEX) or reports, and SME insights. This step may also produce a preliminary view of climate sensitivity by identifying design and operating thresholds linked to meteorological or climate indicators (e.g., precipitation intensities, flood levels, wind speeds, temperature operating ranges, icing conditions, or low-water constraints). Staff “lived experience” is often a useful input at this stage, especially where performance changes or operational challenges have been observed during past weather events.
